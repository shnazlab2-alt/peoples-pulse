## Scene

A city clerk on Friday afternoon has four signed tapes on her desk and a fifth that the machine says it printed. The fifth tape is not in the drawer. The week’s public bulletin already lists Thursday as certified. She can publish Friday’s close on the strength of the machine log, or she can hold the whole week until Thursday’s paper is found. The line for weekend early voting starts at the door. If she certifies the week, the missing Thursday becomes a footnote. If she holds the week, every person in that line pays for a sheet no one else will come back on Saturday to hunt. Who owns a day that the clock remembers and the drawer does not?

## Steelman

The case for publishing Friday on the machine log: a public week that stops the weekend line in order to hunt a sheet no one will read on Saturday is no longer a service. It is a performance of care. The clerk who names the gap in the log, keeps the line moving, and returns to the drawer after the rush is not hiding Thursday. She is refusing to make every stranger at the door pay for a reconciliation the town has already shown it will not attend. A Friday that files the shortfall without pretending the week is signed is the same move.
