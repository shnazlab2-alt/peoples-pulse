# 365 Overlooked Questions

Questions that rarely get asked out loud — in public discourse, in relationships, or in our own heads — but shape more than we admit. Organized into 10 themes, one question per day of the year.

---

## I. Power, Institutions & Governance (37)

1. Who decided the rules you live by, and were you ever asked?
2. What law affects your daily life that you've never actually read?
3. Who benefits when a system is too complicated for ordinary people to understand?
4. What would change if every law came with an expiration date unless renewed?
5. Who gets to decide what counts as "the public interest"?
6. What decisions are being made about you right now in rooms you'll never enter?
7. When an institution apologizes, who actually pays the cost?
8. What would accountability look like if it applied equally to the powerful and the powerless?
9. Who audits the auditors?
10. What happens to power that's never directly voted for?
11. Which of your rights do you only have on paper?
12. What would change if politicians had to live under the laws they pass, immediately?
13. Who decides which crimes get prosecuted and which get "settled"?
14. What institutions do you trust out of habit rather than evidence?
15. Who gets to rewrite history, and how do you know when they have?
16. What would a truly transparent government look like, and would people actually want it?
17. Who is quietly deciding what "normal" means this decade?
18. What happens when a system built to protect people starts protecting itself instead?
19. Who gets a seat at the table when decisions about your community are made?
20. What would change if every regulation had to name a real person accountable for it?
21. Who decided that growth is always the goal?
22. What does it mean to consent to a system you were born into?
23. Who gets to declare an emergency, and what powers does that unlock?
24. What would happen if term limits applied to unelected power too — media, tech, finance?
25. Who is allowed to fail upward, and why?
26. What silent agreements keep unjust systems running smoothly?
27. Who decides what information is classified, and who watches them?
28. What would change if lobbying required the same transparency as voting?
29. Who inherits power without ever earning trust?
30. What happens when the people writing the rules are also the ones breaking them?
31. Who gets blamed when a system fails, and who actually built it?
32. What would a government run by people who wanted to leave power look like?
33. Who decided which borders and boundaries are "natural"?
34. What institutions have outlived the purpose they were built for?
35. Who is allowed to ask questions of power, and who is punished for it?
36. What would justice look like if it wasn't tied to how much you could afford?
37. Who is writing the rules for the next fifty years, and are they thinking past their own lifetime?
