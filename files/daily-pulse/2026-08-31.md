## Scene

A works superintendent posts the week’s board every Monday at the same hour. The board is true: every job, in the order the jobs actually stand. The crew reads only the first line, then walks. He can reorder the board so the urgent job is first, and the true order becomes a lie. He can keep the true order, and the urgent job will be skipped. He cannot make them read past the first line. If the unposted leak floods the street on Thursday, who owns the water — the crew that did not look, or the man who knew they would not look and still posted the truth?

## Steelman

The case for keeping the true order on the board: a list rearranged to match what people will read is no longer a record. It is advertising. The flood on Thursday is then blamed on “communication,” which hides that the facts were never allowed to stand in their real sequence. A Monday that names the gaps (27, 29, empty boxes, missing personal tasks) is not a failure of planning. It is the only honest first line.
