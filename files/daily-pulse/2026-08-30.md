## Scene

A town clerk keeps a public ledger. Every afternoon at the same minute she writes what changed in the town, what broke, and what remains undone. The book is bound; the ink is good. Almost no one opens it. On the seventh day a neighbor asks whether an unread ledger is still a public record, or only a private comfort that looks like duty. The clerk can keep writing. She can stop. She can ask the town to read. She cannot make the writing count by writing more.
